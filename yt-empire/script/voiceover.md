## S3 — THE ENGINE (0:37–1:36)
First — creatine is not a steroid, and it wasn't invented in a lab. Your own body makes about a gram of it every day, and you eat more of it in meat and fish. Ninety-five percent of it is stored inside your muscles as phosphocreatine. Think of every muscle cell as having a tiny battery called ATP. One hard rep, and that battery is drained in seconds. Phosphocreatine is the backup charger — it snaps a fresh phosphate onto the dead battery and buys you two, maybe three more explosive seconds before the muscle taps out. That's the entire magic trick: a bigger backup charger. Supplementing pushes your stores up by twenty to forty percent. More stored energy, more reps at the same weight. And that's where the real growth comes from — but not on day one. Here's what actually happens.

## S4 — THE TIMELINE (1:36–2:29) *(re-hook ~30% hit at S3 close)*
Days one to three: you feel nothing. Creatine is not caffeine — there is no buzz. It's quietly saturating your muscles. Then, around week one, the scale jumps — one to two kilos, almost overnight. Relax: that is not fat. It's water being pulled inside your muscle cells, and that's exactly where you want it — a fuller muscle cell is itself a signal for growth. Week two to three: your stores hit full, and this is where you grind out an extra rep or two on your last set. Feels small. It isn't. Week four and beyond: those extra reps compound into extra volume, and extra volume becomes measurable extra muscle over months. Creatine doesn't build muscle directly — it lets you do more of the thing that builds muscle.

## S5 — MYTH EXECUTIONS (2:29–3:21) *(re-hook ~60%)*
But here's where it gets weird. Creatine gets blamed for kidney damage, hair loss, and bloating — so let's kill all three. Kidneys: in healthy people, three decades of studies have found no damage. The myth survives because creatine slightly raises a lab marker called creatinine — the exact number doctors use to flag kidney stress. Looks scary on a blood test. It isn't. Hair loss: one single study from two thousand nine found a rise in a hormone linked to balding — in twenty rugby players — and it has never been replicated. Ever. Bloating: creatine pulls water into the muscle, not under your skin. If you look puffy, check your sodium — don't blame the powder.
